05
Delivery methodology
How should the solution be delivered?
Sequential · Plan-driven
Waterfall
Requirements↓Design↓Build↓Test↓Deploy

Clear phases · upfront planning · changes can be expensive later · suitable when scope and requirements are stable.

Iterative · Adaptive
Agile
Plan→Build→Review→Adapt↻

Smaller increments · frequent feedback · adapts to changing requirements · value delivered progressively.

01ScrumAgile framework using time-boxed Sprints, defined accountabilities and regular reviews.
02KanbanVisualise work, limit work-in-progress and manage continuous flow.
03LeanMaximise customer value and remove activities that do not add value.
04DevOpsBring development and IT operations together for reliable, continuous delivery.

Scrum is an Agile framework. Kanban and Lean are approaches to managing and improving work; DevOps is a set of practices and culture for development and operations. The JD does not prescribe a specific delivery methodology.

Consulting toolkit
Structure the problem before jumping to a solution.
ConceptWhat it meansExample · loan process
Process mappingVisualise each step in the current process.Map application → document check → credit review → approval.
SIPOCSuppliers, Inputs, Process, Outputs, Customers.Identify who supplies applicant documents and who receives the loan decision.
SwimlaneShow steps by team or role.Separate customer, operations, credit and approval activities.
5 WhysAsk why repeatedly to investigate a root cause.Why is approval delayed? Missing documents; why are they missing? Unclear checklist.
FishboneGroup possible causes of a problem.Explore people, process, technology, data and policy causes of delays.
ParetoIdentify the few causes responsible for most impact.Find which delay category accounts for the largest share of late applications.
As-is / To-beCurrent process compared with desired future process.Manual document checks → structured digital verification workflow.
Functional requirementsWhat the solution must do.System must route a verified application to the credit team.
Non-functional requirementsHow the solution must perform.Secure access, reliable availability and acceptable response time.
ROI / Business caseCompare expected benefits with costs.Estimate hours saved and cost of implementation and maintenance.
KPI / SLA / TATMeasure performance, service commitments and processing time.Track approval turnaround time and percentage completed within SLA.
This is my synthesis of the Digital Delivery model and role description. Technology choices and delivery methods depend on each client's process, requirements and existing environment.
03 / JD terms

The language of the role — and how I understand it.

A quick glossary of the responsibilities, methods and capabilities mentioned in the job description, with simple examples of how they could appear in a client engagement.

≡UNDERSTAND
→ APPLY

01
Finding opportunities
Understand the process before proposing a solution.
JD termWhat I understandSimple example
Opportunity identificationSpot areas where a process could become faster, more accurate, more visible or less costly.Notice that applications wait several days for a manual document check.
Process studyUnderstand the end-to-end steps, people, systems, inputs, outputs and hand-offs.Map an application from submission through verification and approval.
Process assessmentEvaluate how the current process performs and where its limitations are.Measure rework, delays, error rates and pending cases.
Design thinkingExplore the problem from the user's perspective and develop possible solutions.Speak to operations staff to understand why a verification screen is difficult to use, then prototype a simpler flow.
Structured ideationUse a repeatable approach to collect, assess and prioritise improvement ideas.Run an idea session and score suggestions by impact, effort and feasibility.
Top-down / bottom-up assessmentCombine leadership priorities with observations and ideas from people doing the work.Leadership highlights cost reduction; operations staff identify duplicate data entry.
02
Designing the solution
Turn the problem into a practical proposal.
JD termWhat I understandSimple example
Solution conceptualisationShape an initial solution that addresses the problem and fits the client's process and technology environment.Propose a workflow for routing verified applications, with automation for repetitive checks.
Existing IT landscapeUnderstand the applications, databases, integrations, access controls and technology already in use.Check whether applicant data sits in an ATS, CRM or internal database before suggesting another system.
Digital transformationUse technology to improve how a business process works — not simply digitise an unchanged manual step.Redesign a paper-based approval process as a trackable digital workflow.
WorkflowDefine the sequence, routing, approvals, hand-offs and exception paths for work.Send a complete application to the reviewer and return incomplete cases for correction.
RPAUse software bots to perform repetitive, rules-based actions across applications.Copy approved data from one system into another using configured rules.
AI conceptsUse AI capabilities where interpretation, prediction, classification or content generation is useful.Extract key fields from documents or classify incoming requests for human review.
Lean / Six SigmaLean focuses on customer value and reducing waste; Six Sigma focuses on reducing process variation and defects.Remove duplicate approval steps; analyse why document errors repeatedly occur.
Value for moneyShow that the expected business benefit justifies the cost, effort and risk of the solution.Compare implementation and maintenance costs with hours saved and fewer processing errors.
ROI / business caseSet out costs, expected benefits, assumptions, risks and the basis for investment.Estimate annual hours saved, cost avoided and the time needed to recover implementation costs.
03
Working with people and delivery teams
Get agreement, document clearly and support implementation.
JD termWhat I understandSimple example
Stakeholder communicationWork with the people affected by the process or solution, understand their needs and keep them aligned.Discuss requirements with operations, IT, compliance and the client sponsor.
Stakeholder buy-in / approvalsExplain the proposed change, address concerns and obtain agreement before moving forward.Present the proposed workflow, cost and expected benefits for approval.
CxOsSenior executives such as CEOs, CFOs and COOs; communication should connect the proposal to business priorities.Explain how a change could improve turnaround time, cost or service quality.
Functional requirementsSpecify what the solution must do.The system must assign each complete request to the correct approval queue.
Non-functional requirementsSpecify how well or under what conditions the solution must operate.Define security, availability, performance, scalability and auditability expectations.
Development team liaisonAct as a bridge between business stakeholders and the people building the solution; clarify requirements and resolve questions.Explain an approval rule to developers and confirm how exceptions should behave.
UAT — User Acceptance TestingSupport users in checking that the solution meets agreed business requirements before it goes live.Test normal, incomplete and exception cases with operations users and record defects.
Cross-functional teamPeople from different functions working towards the same outcome.Business analysts, operations, developers, testers, data teams and client stakeholders.
04
Measuring and communicating impact
Check whether the solution delivered what it promised.
JD termWhat I understandSimple example
Benefits realisationTrack whether the expected improvements actually occur after implementation.Compare processing time and error rates before and after automation.
Reporting & MISProvide regular management information on operational performance and progress.A weekly dashboard showing volume, pending cases, turnaround time and SLA achievement.
CoE — Centre of ExcellenceA specialist group that develops standards, expertise, reusable practices and support for a capability.Share a consistent reporting format or automation best practices across delivery teams.
Case studyDocument the business problem, solution, implementation and measurable outcome.Show how a redesigned workflow reduced average processing time.
Client testimonialCapture the client's feedback on the value and experience of the delivered solution.A client sponsor describes the improvement in visibility and service levels.
Solution championBuild confidence in a proposal, explain its value clearly and help stakeholders move towards adoption.Present the problem, recommendation, expected benefits and evidence in a clear story.
Basic programmingUnderstand programming logic and be able to follow or work with simple code; the JD describes this as a significant plus.Understand variables, conditions, loops and basic SQL or scripting used to handle data.

How I read the role overall: understand a business process, identify an improvement opportunity, shape a technology-enabled solution, align stakeholders, support requirements and testing, and help measure the benefits. The examples above are illustrative explanations of the JD terms, not claims about a specific Infosys client project.
