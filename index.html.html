01 / My understanding

Digital Delivery is where business processes meet technology.

Infosys BPM describes Digital Delivery as its software technology arm: enabling, optimising and transforming client business processes through domain-specific platforms and solutions.

↗PROCESS
＋ TECHNOLOGY
＋ PEOPLE
01
The business
First, understand the work.
◎Business processHow work moves through an organisation
◷TimeWhere does work slow down?
⌁QualityWhere do errors happen?
⇄HandoffsWho depends on whom?
◫VisibilityCan teams see progress?

My understanding is that Infosys BPM works with organisations across business functions to run and improve processes. Digital Delivery is the technology enabler: it develops and implements Workflow, Reporting, RPA and AI solutions to improve efficiency and productivity across domains. It also identifies improvement opportunities through structured ideation programmes, top-down and bottom-up process assessments, and Lean / Six Sigma methodologies. The starting point is not choosing a tool. It is understanding the process, the people involved and the outcome the business needs.

05
Ways of working
How digital solutions move from idea to implementation.
Plan-driven
Waterfall
Requirements↓Design↓Build↓Test↓Deploy

Sequential phases with substantial upfront planning. Useful when scope, dependencies and acceptance criteria are relatively stable or tightly governed.

Iterative & adaptive
Agile
Plan→Build→Review→Adapt↻

Work is delivered in smaller increments, with regular feedback and reprioritisation as teams learn more about user needs.

A common Agile framework
Scrum · short, focused work sprints.

In Scrum, a cross-functional team works towards a Sprint Goal during a time-boxed Sprint, commonly one to four weeks. The team inspects progress and adapts its plan regularly.

Product OwnerMaximises product value and orders the Product Backlog.
Scrum MasterEstablishes Scrum, coaches the team and helps remove impediments.
DevelopersPlan and deliver a usable Increment each Sprint.
Backlog refinement→Sprint planning→Daily Scrum→Review→Retrospective
ILLUSTRATIVE CLIENT EXAMPLE · HR SERVICES FIRM

A first Sprint might focus only on automating interview reminders. The team could demo it to recruiters, gather feedback, then prioritise onboarding alerts in a later Sprint. A larger, tightly controlled system migration might instead use more plan-driven stages—or a hybrid approach.

Agile and Waterfall are delivery approaches; Scrum is a framework commonly used to put Agile principles into practice. The JD does not prescribe one delivery methodology.

01
Supervised learning

Trains on labelled examples—input data paired with the correct outcome.

Common tasksClassification · Regression
Business usePredict candidate drop-off risk; classify support tickets; forecast hiring time.
02
Unsupervised learning

Finds patterns in unlabelled data, without a supplied target answer.

Common tasksClustering · Dimensionality reduction · Anomaly detection
Business useGroup candidates by skill profiles; identify unusual process patterns or outliers.
03
Reinforcement learning

An agent learns by taking actions and receiving rewards or penalties from its environment.

Common tasksSequential decisions · Optimisation
Business useExplore dynamic resource allocation or scheduling where decisions affect later outcomes.
GENERATIVE AILarge Language ModelsDraft, summarise, classify and answer questions about text; e.g. assist with job-description summaries or recruiter knowledge search.
GENERATIVE AIMultimodal modelsWork across combinations of text, images, audio and video; e.g. extract information from varied document formats—with validation.
AI IN OPERATIONSHuman-in-the-loopPeople review sensitive or consequential outputs, handle exceptions and provide feedback.
ILLUSTRATIVE CLIENT EXAMPLE · HR SERVICES FIRM

With historical labelled hiring data, supervised learning might estimate time-to-fill. Without labelled categories, unsupervised clustering could reveal groups of similar requisitions. A generative AI assistant could summarise a job description or recruiter notes—but should not independently make hiring decisions. Data privacy, bias, explainability and human oversight matter.

“Types of AI” can mean different taxonomies. The practical categories above describe learning approaches and capabilities used in business. Narrow AI / Artificial General Intelligence / superintelligence is a separate capability-based discussion; AGI and superintelligence remain theoretical rather than ordinary enterprise tools.

So, the way I see Digital Delivery is: understand the business, improve the process, use technology thoughtfully, bring people along and measure whether the change made a difference.

This is my synthesis of Infosys BPM's business and digital delivery model, not a claim that every engagement follows an identical sequence or uses every technology.
